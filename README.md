## Working Principles

### Begin with the operating reality

Technology should reflect how the organization, its industry, and its people actually work. Procedures, standards, obligations, economics, and service expectations are architectural requirements—not background information.

### Make the work easier to do well

Good systems reduce friction, improve visibility and coordination, and lower avoidable cost without transferring additional complexity to the people they are meant to support.

### Design the whole system

Technology does not operate independently of people, processes, incentives, vendors, policies, data, or organizational history. Durable solutions account for those relationships from the beginning.

### Keep states visible

Proposed, approved, implemented, tested, deployed, verified, and accepted are different conditions. Systems should preserve those distinctions rather than creating the appearance of progress.

### Prefer evidence to assertion

Important work should leave a reliable trail: requirements, decisions, ownership, validation, documentation, and proof of outcome.

### Use AI with judgment

AI can accelerate research, analysis, development, and execution. It does not eliminate the need for context, professional judgment, validation, accountability, or human responsibility.

### Treat documentation as infrastructure

Documentation is not an administrative afterthought. It preserves knowledge, strengthens continuity, supports collaboration, and makes complex work easier to understand and improve.
